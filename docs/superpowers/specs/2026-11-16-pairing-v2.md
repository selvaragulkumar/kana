# Pairing v2: rows that are never wrong

Status: implemented on branch `kana-pairing-v2` (2026-11-16). Supersedes the pairing rules of
`kana/pairing.py` v1 (kept as `kana/pairing_v1.py` for comparison).

## Intent

The transcript panel shows each source sentence beside its translation. On the 85-minute
customer call of 2026-11-16 only about 35–40 % of inbound rows showed the right Japanese beside
the right English: 337 of 819 rows were English only, and a third of the rest paired a long
Japanese sentence with an unrelated short translation ("Yes."). The audio was fine; the panel
was not trustworthy. A row the user cannot trust is worse than no row.

## What the data says

Measured with the events log on synthetic speech through the real model
(`docs/research/transcription-timing.md`):

- Live Translate streams the input transcription about one chunk per second while the person
  speaks, and the output transcription of each chunk follows 0.26–0.40 s later. The streams
  interleave 1:1.
- It sends no `turn_complete`, `generation_complete` or `interrupted`, and never sets
  `finished`.
- The 20–30 s "late source" was the engine's sentence buffer: v1 received the Japanese only at
  its next `。`, which in run-on speech came up to 32 s (synthetic) or minutes (the call) later,
  and then gave it the translation that came next. One such event shifted every later row.

The hypothesis in the brief (group by the model's turn signals, keep translation-only rows open
for the measured lag, split a late N:1 chunk across them) is therefore only half right: there
are no turns, and with chunks fed directly there is no lag to wait for. The split of a late
chunk is kept for providers that do send one.

## Goals

1. What the user sees is never wrong: a source fragment is never shown beside a translation that
   is not its own. A row may be big (one long Japanese turn with several English sentences); it
   may show the English first and the Japanese a moment later.
2. Both sides appear as they stream: no side is held back waiting for a sentence end.
3. Nothing is dropped: every chunk of either side lands in exactly one row, in order.
4. No protocol change: the same `transcript` op (`kind`, `text`, `row`, `row_text`), so the app
   needs no change.

## Non-goals

- Sentence-level alignment inside a long turn (one row per Japanese sentence). The model's
  English sentences do not map 1:1 onto Japanese sentences; forcing it is what made v1 wrong.
- Merging or hiding back-channel rows (はい / "Yes."): a separate, display-side proposal.
- Changing what is translated or the voice.

## Anti-goals

- Re-pairing based on the content of the text (translation-quality heuristics, a second model
  call). Pairing stays a function of arrival order, timing and length.
- Moving text between rows after they are shown, except appending to a row. (v1's `merge_into`
  is no longer produced; the app still understands it.)

## Design

### Input

`Direction._add` passes every transcription chunk to the pairer as it arrives (`Pairer.chunks =
True`), with its arrival time and utterance number. The sentence buffer stays only for the
terminal output, the utterance bookkeeping and latency. A pairer without `chunks` (v1) is still
fed sentences, so the replay harness can compare both on the same call.

### Rules

A translation chunk translates the source chunk that came just before it (finding 2).

Translation chunk:

- **T1** The last row's translation has not ended a sentence: append to it. It may be a row a
  source chunk opened a moment ago (the usual case: source first, translation 0.3 s later).
- **T2** The last row's translation is done, but the latest source chunk went into that row
  after it was done (a tail, S5, or a source that filled an early translation, S1/S2) less than
  `LEAD_S` = 2 s ago (measured lead: 0.26–0.52 s), or the row's source stops mid-clause: append.
  The model is still translating that source in a second English sentence. The mid-clause case
  stops once the row's source is 160 CJK characters long; the tail case has no bound (the third
  recording showed why: a bound there shifted the next rows by one).
  T2 also heals a wrong guess: a source put beside the wrong translation (S2) pulls its own
  translation in after it, so the row grows instead of the next rows shifting.
- **T3** Otherwise: a new row.

A translation that ends with `. ? !` marks its row done.

Source chunk:

- **S1 Late source.** Two or more rows at the end have a translation and no source (translated
  less than `LATE_S` = 30 s ago): split the chunk after each clause end (`、。？！,.?!`) and assign
  the pieces to those rows in order by a monotonic length alignment (dynamic programming over
  contiguous groups, cost = |log length ratio|). Never to a row after them.
- **S2** The last row has a translation and no source: append (an early translation).
- **S3** The last row's translation is not done: append. Two exceptions: the row has a
  translation and both streams were silent for `GAP_S` = 3 s, then the chunk opens a new row; or
  the row is source only, got no translation for `DROP_S` = 5 s and its source is at a clause
  end, then it is given up (untranslated) and the chunk opens a new row.
- **S4** The last row's translation is done and its source ends at a clause end: a new row; the
  translation that follows joins it (T1).
- **S5** The row's source stopped mid-clause (a Japanese verb-final tail such as `…ます。` comes
  after the English sentence ended): the chunk's text up to its first clause end goes to that row
  if it comes within `TAIL_S` = 2.5 s of the translation's end and the tail stays within 12 CJK
  characters; the rest opens a new row. A long or late chunk opens a new row whole.

Sealing: a row is final (saved) `SEAL_S` = 12 s after its last change. A row with a translation
and no source waits `LATE_S` = 30 s, so a late source can still fill it. The oldest row seals
first, as before.

Display text: chunks are concatenated as sent (they carry their own spaces); a space is added
where an English chunk starts a new sentence without one, and spaces next to Japanese text are
removed (the call's transcript had `ね 、`).

### Why this is never wrong

The only decision is where a row ends. A row ends where the English ends a sentence and the
Japanese has reached a clause end, or on 3 s of silence. If the Japanese has not reached a
clause end, the row grows (T2, S5) instead of cutting: a row can be too big, never mismatched.
Alignment is re-anchored at every chunk by arrival order, so one odd event cannot shift later
rows (v1's cascade).

### Unchanged

The `untranslated` event: a row whose source got no translation when its utterance closes after
1.5 s of quiet. The app's backstop for pending rows. The saved Markdown and SRT (one line per
row).

## Protocol

No field changes. One semantic change, documented in `docs/ENGINE.md`: a `transcript` event now
carries one chunk (or part of one) as it arrives, not a finished sentence; `row_text` is still
the whole side, which is all the app uses. `app/fake_engine.py` now streams rows chunk by chunk
the same way, and `tests/test_events.py` checks it. The Swift app needs no change: it already
sets `row_text` by row id.

## Tests

- `tests/test_pairing_v2.py`: synthetic sequences (our own sentences) for a run-on turn,
  back-channel rows, a split translation, a verb-final tail, the translation of a tail, an early
  translation, a late N:1 source chunk, the `LATE_S` bound, a dropped translation, silence, a
  long or late tail, sealing, display spacing and the alignment; the v1 cascade on the same
  speech fed as sentences; Direction feeding chunks; terminal output unchanged.
- Replays of the three recorded Live Translate sessions (`tests/data/*.events.jsonl`): every
  row with English has Japanese, every hand-checked anchor pair is in one row (19/19, 18/18,
  17/17; v1: 9/19, 7/18, 10/17), the back-channel rows are exact, and every chunk is in the rows
  in order. The third was recorded with the first v2 live, which shifted two rows; its replay
  is a regression test for rule T2.

## Done means

- The suite passes (`.venv/bin/python -m pytest -q`).
- The replays above hold.
- On the next real call, the events log is saved, and a replay of it shows no row whose Japanese
  is beside a translation that is not its own (check by hand on a sample; then add it as an
  anonymised fixture only if the content allows).
